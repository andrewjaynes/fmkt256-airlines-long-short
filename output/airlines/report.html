How to reuse this: new companies or new data, zero new code

Business

Largest U.S. airline by revenue ($65.6B TTM), with seven U.S. hubs including Newark, Chicago, Denver, Houston and San Francisco, and the deepest Pacific and Atlantic network. Premium cabins and MileagePlus drive margin.

Latest quarter & guidance

  • Q3 2026 revenue was a record $17.9B (+17.4% YoY) on higher yields; GAAP net income $1.03B, adjusted EPS $2.54 (-8.6%).
  • Fuel expense rose 61.6% to $4.84B as the average price hit $3.90/gal vs $2.43 a year earlier, with gallons roughly flat.
  • Q4 revenue growth guided above 20%, but per Skift, Q4 adjusted EPS guidance of $0.50-$1.50 (at $4.80/gal fuel) sits well below the $1.76 consensus.

Strengths

  • Highest ROE in the group (22%) and #2 net margin
  • $17.7B of available liquidity, the largest cushion of the peers
  • Pricing power: Q4 revenue expected up more than 20%

Risks

  • $26.4B of debt and finance leases; free cash flow negative (-$0.6B TTM)
  • Highly exposed to jet fuel staying near $4.80/gal
  • International demand sensitive to the Middle East conflict

Catalysts (6–12 months)

  • Q4 2026 results and 2027 guide in January
  • Any easing in crude/jet fuel prices
  • Continued premium and loyalty revenue growth

Business

The largest U.S. regional airline (522 regional jets in service or under contract), flying as United Express, Delta Connection, American Eagle and Alaska. Most flying is under capacity-purchase agreements (CPAs) where the major partner pays a fixed fee and bears fuel cost.

Latest quarter & guidance

  • Q3 2026 diluted EPS of $3.08, up from $2.81 a year earlier, on revenue of $1.12B (reported Oct 29).
  • Higher fuel prices hurt only the smaller prorate (revenue-sharing) segment, where SkyWest does bear fuel.
  • Repurchased $75M of stock in Q3 with ~$238M of authorization left; 69 more E175s are scheduled for delivery, mostly to partner contracts.

Strengths

  • Business model largely passes fuel cost to partners
  • Cheapest stock in the group: EV/EBITDA 5.3x, forward P/E 7.6x
  • Highest margins (14% operating, 9.8% net)

Risks

  • Concentrated customers: pressured majors could cut regional flying
  • Prorate segment does carry fuel risk
  • Pilot supply and contract renewal terms

Catalysts (6–12 months)

  • Q4 results and placement of 33 still-unassigned E175 deliveries
  • Re-rating as the market recognizes fuel insulation
  • Ongoing buybacks ($238M authorized)

Methodology, sources and AI prompts

Quantitative data

All market and financial figures are pulled live from Yahoo Finance (via the open-source yfinance library) each time the report is generated. No numbers are typed in by hand. TTM margins are the sum of the last four reported quarters. Revenue growth compares the latest quarter with the same quarter a year earlier. Net debt = total debt (incl. leases) − cash and investments.

Composite score

Eleven metrics are converted to percentile ranks within the peer group (so the score is always relative), averaged into five pillars, then weighted per the sector file:

PillarMetrics (direction)Weight
ValuationEV/EBITDA ↓, forward P/E ↓25%
ProfitabilityOperating margin ↑, net margin ↑, ROE ↑20%
Balance sheetNet debt/EBITDA ↓, cash/revenue ↑25%
Momentum1-yr return ↑, distance from 52-wk high ↑15%
Street viewUpside to consensus target ↑, short interest ↓15%

Negative EBITDA or earnings make a multiple "not meaningful"; those companies receive the lowest score on that metric.

Division of labor: code vs. AI

Code does what must be exact: fetching, calculating, ranking, charting. AI does what needs judgment: researching recent earnings and news, writing the qualitative assessment, and making the final long/short call (it may override the screen if it explains why).
